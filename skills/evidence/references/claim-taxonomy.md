# Claim Status and Provenance

Use the narrowest status established by the available evidence.

| Status | Required support | Required communication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified fact | Direct observation or authoritative source with scope and observation time | State the observed scope; do not generalize beyond it. |
| Derived result | Cited inputs and a reproducible formula or procedure | Name the inputs and method. |
| Inference | Cited facts plus an inspectable explanatory link | Distinguish it from direct observation and preserve plausible alternatives. |
| Hypothesis | A falsifiable explanation or prediction | Name the observation that would refute it. |
| Estimate | Declared assumptions and uncertainty appropriate to its use | Avoid presenting a point value as measured. |
| Unknown | Missing or conflicting evidence | Name what is missing, why it matters, and the cheapest useful next observation. |
| Decision | Alternatives, criteria, evidence, owner, and validation plan | Present it as a choice, not a fact. |

## Quantitative provenance

A material number is supportable only when the record includes its metric and unit, population or
denominator, observation window, source and revision or inspection date, collection or derivation
method, exclusions, assumptions, and material limitations.

A benchmark applies only to its recorded hardware, dataset, configuration, warm-up, and repetitions.
A passing test establishes the exercised cases, not absence of defects. A configured service does
not establish reachability, authentication, or health. A transcript establishes what was said, not
that the statement remains current.
